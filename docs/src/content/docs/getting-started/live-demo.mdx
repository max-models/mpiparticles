This demo runs mpiparticles in your browser with [Pyodide](https://pyodide.org), on the NumPy
versions of its kernels (there is no compiler in the browser). Move the sliders to push the
markers again:

A `ParticleStore` holds 100 markers of each species, with charge over mass +1 and −4 (in
normalized units) and thermal velocities, in an electric field along y and a magnetic field
along z. `boris_push` moves them, with one charge over mass per marker; `boris_velocity`
first steps their velocities half a step back to start the leapfrog.

The two species gyrate in opposite directions and on different radii, but drift together
along x at E/B: the numbers below the figures compare the measured drift speeds with it. The
Boris scheme gives this drift exactly even when the gyration is under-resolved: raise the time
step until ω<sub>c</sub>Δt of the lighter species is well above 1: its steps jump across the
gyration, but its drift stays at E/B.
